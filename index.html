You've Been Preparing for Months. But Do You Know Exactly Where You Stand?

Most students in your position aren't lacking effort. They're lacking a clear, honest picture of where they actually stand.

Hours of study don't always translate to visible progress.

You cover a topic, practice a few problems, and move on. But there's rarely a clear way to confirm whether it's actually sunk in — until a later test brings back something you thought you already knew.

Marks and real understanding aren't always the same thing.

It's possible to clear a test with a good score, and still find yourself unsure when asked to explain the same concept out loud — in a viva, an interview, or a technical discussion. Recall and understanding are measured differently, but most tests only check one of them.

The more you revise, the harder it gets to tell "familiar" from "understood."

After enough repetition, almost everything starts to feel comfortable. Lectures, notes, and practice sets are designed to help you cover ground — but they don't tell you, individually, which of the hundred concepts you've covered are actually solid, and which just feel familiar because you've seen them often. That distinction only shows up when someone checks the concept itself, not just whether you've been exposed to it.

Without a clear picture, everything feels equally important.

When you don't know exactly which concepts are weak, it's hard to prioritize. You end up spending equal time everywhere — revising what you already know well, and unintentionally skipping what actually needs the work.

You don't need to try harder. You need to know exactly where to point the effort you're already putting in.

THE SOLUTION

A Diagnostic Built to Check Understanding — Not Just Track Coverage

Most preparation — classes, notes, practice sets, mock tests — is designed to help you cover the syllabus and measure how much you remember. That's valuable, and it's necessary. But it answers a different question than the one that actually matters before an exam or interview: not "how much have I covered," but "what do I genuinely understand, and where exactly am I still weak."

[Diagnostic Name] is built specifically to answer that second question. Instead of a single score, it looks at the evidence in how you respond — question by question, concept by concept — and builds an individual picture of your understanding: what's genuinely strong, what's average, what's weak, and what hasn't been tested clearly enough to tell either way.

The result isn't another score to track. It's a clear, honest map of where you stand today, and exactly what to focus on next.

A Few Things Students Usually Ask

Is this only for C programming, or does it cover other subjects too?

It covers Core CS subjects broadly — including DSA, OS, DBMS, OOP, and more. Programming languages like C are supported where a subject requires code-based questions, but the diagnostic isn't limited to any single language or topic.

Is this specifically for GATE preparation?

Right now, the diagnostic is focused on GATE-relevant Core CS subjects. It's built on the same underlying concept structure we'll be extending to technical interviews, PYQs, and other areas over time — so it's designed to grow with your preparation, not just one exam.

How long does the diagnostic take?

It depends on how many subjects and concepts you choose to cover, since there's no fixed timer per question. Most students complete a subject-level diagnostic in a single sitting or across two to three shorter sessions, using auto-save to pick up where they left off.

Is this free?

Yes, starting the diagnostic is free.

Will this replace my regular classes or coaching?

No — it's designed to work alongside whatever preparation you're already doing. Classes and practice sets help you cover material; this is meant to show you, individually, which parts of that material you've actually understood.

What if I don't finish in one sitting?

Your progress is saved automatically as you go. You can close the diagnostic anytime and resume later from exactly where you left off.

Do I need to prepare or revise before taking the diagnostic?

No preparation is needed. In fact, taking it before revising can be more useful — it gives you an honest starting picture, so your revision time goes toward what actually needs it.
